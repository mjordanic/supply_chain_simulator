# RL training environment: randomised assortment, slot-shuffled observations, hidden market state, frozen assortment within episode

The RL training environment wraps the existing `Market` / `EventEngine` / `ItemRegistry` / `Store` subsystems in Gymnasium step-by-step semantics. Four design decisions shape how episodes are constructed and how the agent perceives the world.

**Decision 1 — Randomised assortment per episode.** Each `reset()` draws K active SKUs uniformly without replacement from the full 100-product catalog universe. With K = 5 from a 100-product catalog there are ~75 million unique assortments, making per-assortment memorisation infeasible. We chose this over (a) **fixed assortment across all episodes** — which would let the agent memorise per-SKU strategies rather than learning transferable pricing and ordering principles; (b) **stratified sampling** (e.g. one per category) — which reduces variability across the assortment composition and could be added later as curriculum without changing the env contract; (c) **per-product factored policy** (separate policy head per SKU) — which scales poorly to large catalogs and conflicts with the goal of a single policy that generalises. The uniform draw combined with the slot-shuffle (see Decision 2) ensures the agent cannot exploit product identity.

**Decision 2 — Slot-shuffled observation encoding.** The K active SKUs are placed in K observation slots in a per-episode random permutation of their catalog positions. The permutation is drawn from the episode RNG and is fixed for the duration of the episode; the decoder applies the inverse permutation when mapping action slots back to product ids. We chose this over (a) **fixed identity slots** (slot i always carries the SKU with the i-th catalog id) — which would allow the agent to associate position with product identity and learn product-specific rather than feature-driven strategies; (b) **set-based attention** (no fixed slots; a transformer over the K SKU feature vectors) — which provides true permutation invariance but adds architectural complexity; the slot-shuffle achieves the same CRN-disjoint invariance guarantee within the MLP actor-critic without attention layers. A future switch to a set-encoder is a class-level change: only `Actor` / `Critic` need to change; the encoder contract is unchanged.

**Decision 3 — Hidden regional market state.** The `market_demand` and `market_supply` regional scalars are deliberately excluded from the observation, even though they are available inside the simulator. The agent must infer demand conditions from realised sales, inventory depletion, and stockout signals. We chose this over (a) **exposing market state** — which would give the agent privileged information unavailable to a real store manager, making learned policies non-transferable to the real world and invalidating the paired comparison with `BaselinePolicy` (which also operates without regional state); (b) **partial exposure** (e.g. lagged or noisy market signal) — adding implementation complexity for an effect achievable by training on diverse episodes. Lifecycle stage and calendar encoding are exposed because a real store manager can observe product maturity and seasonality.

**Decision 4 — Frozen assortment within an episode.** The `activate` and `deactivate` action keys are forced to empty lists for the entire episode. Catalog rotation decisions are deferred to a follow-up. We chose this over (a) **per-tick activate/deactivate** — which would require a separate discrete head on the actor, expand the action space, and make the learning problem harder before the simpler price/order problem is solved; (b) **per-K-tick rotation** (decide assortment every K steps, prices and orders every step) — a natural extension but also best added once the base policy is working. The simulator already supports `activate_item` / `deactivate_item` so no new sim machinery is required when the rotation head is added in a follow-up.
